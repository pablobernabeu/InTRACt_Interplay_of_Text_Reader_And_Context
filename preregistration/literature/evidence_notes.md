# Evidence notes behind the protocol's less obvious choices

Working notes, August 2026. Each entry records the evidence that fixed a decision in the
protocol, with the source it rests on. Bibliographic metadata for every DOI below was checked
against Crossref; where a claim rests on a reading of the paper rather than on its abstract,
that is said. These notes are the justification record, not part of the submission.

## Why Hypotheses 2 and 3 are two-sided adjudicating tests, not directional predictions

The earlier draft predicted developmental attenuation for both interactions. The direct
evidence does not support either sign, and on the log scale the study uses, part of it points
the other way.

**Grammatical load × age band (H2).**

- Wonnacott, Joseph, Adelman and Nation (2016, doi:10.1080/17470218.2015.1011176) is the
  best-powered direct test over exactly this contrast (Year 3, 7;7–9;1, n = 42, against
  Year 6, 10;0–11;6, n = 39): a large garden-path effect with *no* age interaction (t = 0.36).
  Caveat: a temporary-ambiguity manipulation indexes reanalysis, not the sustained maintenance
  that dependency-distance measures load on, so this null does not transfer automatically.
- Joseph and Liversedge (2013, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0054141) found attenuation confined to
  late measures, at n = 14 per group. Decisive for the scale argument: expressed as a
  proportion of baseline, the complexity effect *grew* with age (roughly 15, 25 and 31 per
  cent in younger children, older children and adults). Log reading time makes coefficients
  proportional, so the experience account predicts a positive interaction here.
- Faust, Balota, Spieler and Ferraro (1999, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.777): purely
  multiplicative group slowing manufactures a spurious over-additive interaction on raw
  time. This is why the log scale is described in the protocol as load-bearing.
- The capacity account's positive evidence in this band is correlational and largely auditory
  (Delage and Frauenfelder 2019, doi:10.1075/lia.18013.del); Peng et al.'s meta-analysis
  (2018, doi:10.1037/bul0000124) found the working-memory relation to comprehension largely
  carried by decoding and vocabulary. The experience account (MacDonald and Christiansen
  2002, doi:10.1037/0033-295X.109.1.35; Montag and MacDonald 2015, doi:10.1037/xge0000054)
  derives the same developmental main effects without capacity.
- Booth, MacWhinney and Harasaki (2000, doi:10.1111/1467-8624.00203) report a non-monotonic
  pattern for relative clauses across 8 to 11, read from the abstract only; the full text
  was not obtained. Not cited for more than the possibility of non-monotonicity.

**Referential continuity × age band (H3).**

- Cain and Nash (2011, doi:10.1037/a0022824), Experiment 4: 8- and 10-year-olds gained the
  same self-paced speed-up from an appropriate connective; the age interaction on reading
  time is explicitly null. Their general discussion itself warns the sign may reverse for
  expository reading-to-learn material.
- Helder, Van Leijenhorst and van den Broek (2016, doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2016.02.008): online
  coherence-break detection did not differ between 8-to-9 and 10-to-11-year-olds.
- The child "reverse cohesion" demonstration (McNamara, Ozuru and Floyd 2011, no registered
  DOI; International Electronic Journal of Elementary Education, 4(1), 229–257) is a
  world-knowledge effect within one grade, on recall only, for narrative only, N = 65, and
  decoding skill moderated nothing. It licenses no age-based prediction.
- The skill side runs the other way: O'Reilly and McNamara (2007,
  doi:10.1080/01638530709336895) found skilled comprehenders benefited from *high* cohesion.
  Between 7 and 11 knowledge and skill rise together, so construction-integration predicts
  partial cancellation of unknown net sign.
- No study has manipulated whole-text coherence in this age band and measured reading rate.
  That cell of the literature is empty, which is stated in the protocol as the reason any
  determinate outcome is informative.
- Kleijn, Pander Maat and Sanders (2019, doi:10.1080/0163853X.2019.1605257; 20 texts,
  n = 794): causal and contrastive
  connectives raised comprehension, temporal did nothing, additive lowered it. This fixed
  the rule that connectives enter the component by relation type with causal and contrastive
  anchors.
- Length confound: standard high-cohesion versions run 20 to 40 per cent longer than their
  low-cohesion counterparts, so a whole-text duration would show coherence slowing reading
  for arithmetic reasons. This fixed per-word time as the only outcome scale.

## Why Hypothesis 4 carries a compression diagnostic and a non-binding-audio subset

- The selective-decoding account has the mechanism evidence: a partial read-aloud
  accommodation helped poor decoders while a pacing-only control did nothing (Giusto and
  Ehri 2019, doi:10.1177/0022219418789377); the benefit concentrates in poor decoding with
  intact language (Keelor et al. 2023, doi:10.1007/s11881-023-00281-9); the time saved falls
  on first encounters with orthographically novel words and is gone by the second
  (Valentini et al. 2024, doi:10.1002/rrq.522).
- The pacing account has the outcome evidence: the reading-while-listening benefit is
  confined to externally paced comparisons (Clinton-Lisell 2023, no registered DOI, OSF
  10.31219/osf.io/ku6tv; Clinton-Lisell 2022, doi:10.3102/00346543211060871); a preregistered
  mechanism test found comprehension worse under narration, attributed to the forced pace
  blocking rereading (Hui and Godfroid 2026, doi:10.1111/lang.12721); and measured
  incidentally, narration collapsed the standard deviation of exposure time from 11.56 s to
  0.62 s (Valentini et al. 2024).
- Nobody has regressed reading time on decoding-level and integration-level predictors
  separately under both narration states. H4 is that decomposition; the residual-variance
  ratio and the faster-than-audio subset are the two controls that keep the pacing account
  honest.

## Why the plausibility bounds are 10 and 400 words per minute

- The only published precedent stating such a rule with its yield: Ripoll Salceda, Tapia
  Montesinos and Aguado Alonso (2020, doi:10.1016/j.psicod.2020.01.002) deleted silent rates
  above 400 wpm as exceeding adult capability and indicating skipped text, removing 313 of
  19,237 cases (1.6 per cent). Their pooled child norms also anchor the lower bound: the
  slowest group mean in the literature is about 30 wpm (Spanish grade 1, silent), so a floor
  of 10 wpm excludes only sessions left open.
- Context norms: Hasbrouck and Tindal's compiled oral-fluency norms (2017, ERIC ED594994);
  silent comprehension-based rates rising from about 115 wpm in grade 2 (Hiebert, Samuels
  and Rasinski 2012, doi:10.1080/19388071.2010.531887), with the 2011 replication finding
  substantially lower rates than 1960 (Spichtig et al. 2016, doi:10.1002/rrq.137). Adult
  silent rates across languages fall roughly in 181 to 285 wpm, which motivated 160 wpm as a
  conservative ceiling on any narration rate in H4's non-binding-audio subset.

## Why Hypothesis 5 treats the rating as a measure under test

- No peer-reviewed study was found in which children aged 6 to 12 rated the difficulty of a
  text and the rating was validated against the text's properties; the child metacognition
  literature rates confidence in answers, judgements of learning, or enjoyment.
- The three-point easy / just-right / difficult format is a classroom heuristic with no
  published psychometrics.
- An evidence sweep reported an ordinal alpha of 0.23 for adolescents rating sentence
  complexity, attributed to Thome et al. (2025); the primary record could not be located in
  Crossref, so that figure was excluded from the manuscript and the argument rests on the
  absence of validation evidence alone. Hence the text-level aggregation, the five-rater
  floor, and the pre-registered three-way interpretation grid in which Hypothesis 1
  disambiguates a failure.

## Why the multilingual resource plan changed

- wordfreq is frozen at roughly 2021 and was discontinued in September 2024, its maintainer
  citing generated-text contamination of post-2022 web corpora; its Korean and Greek lists
  are thinner than the rest. Replaced as primary by subs2vec (van Paridon and Thompson 2021,
  doi:10.3758/s13428-020-01406-3), one pipeline over 55 languages.
- The non-English NRC VAD lexicons are 2022 machine translations of the English norms under
  a non-commercial licence; replaced by MEmoLon (Buechel et al. 2020,
  doi:10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.112), induced uniformly for 91 languages.
- LaBSE is optimised for translation-pair retrieval, not within-language similarity;
  replaced by BGE-M3 (Chen et al. 2024, doi:10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.137).
- Goldfish (doi:10.63317/5ceec3hhv4d5) trains an identical architecture on byte-premium-scaled
  data per language, which is what makes surprisal comparable across languages by
  construction; mGPT is the single-model sensitivity.
- Korean lacks a subtitle frequency norm, any coverage in the Łuniewska cross-linguistic
  age-of-acquisition set, indexed concreteness norms and native affect norms. Crossref
  indexes Korean-language journals poorly, so this is recorded as a search limitation and a
  KCI/RISS search by a Korean-reading collaborator is required before the feature inventory
  is frozen.
