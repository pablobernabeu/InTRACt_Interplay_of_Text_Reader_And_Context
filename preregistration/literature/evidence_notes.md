# Evidence notes behind the protocol's less obvious choices

Working notes, August 2026. Each entry records the evidence that fixed a decision in the
protocol, with the source it rests on. Bibliographic metadata for every DOI below was checked
against Crossref; where a claim rests on a reading of the paper rather than on its abstract,
that is said. These notes are the justification record, not part of the submission.

## Why Hypotheses 2 and 3 are two-sided adjudicating tests, not directional predictions

The earlier draft predicted developmental attenuation for both interactions. The direct
evidence does not support either sign, and on the log scale the study uses, part of it points
the other way.

**Which account carries the positive sign (relabelled 2026-09-05).** Until this pass, the
positive interaction was attributed to "the experience account" of MacDonald and Christiansen
(2002, doi:10.1037/0033-295X.109.1.35) and Montag and MacDonald (2015,
doi:10.1037/xge0000054). That attribution was wrong in sign. The canonical test of the
experience-based account is Wells, Christiansen, Race, Acheson and MacDonald (2009,
doi:10.1016/j.cogpsych.2008.08.002): after weeks of relative-clause experience the
object-relative penalty fell from 68 ms to 21 ms, while a control group went from 34 ms to
49 ms. Montag and MacDonald is a production study in which more text exposure goes with more
adult-like complex-sentence production. On both, more experience makes complex structure
*cheaper*, so the experience account shares the capacity account's negative sign. The mechanism
the manuscript actually described, an older reader who "engages with it more fully and pays a
proportionally larger cost", is depth of structure building, which belongs to the good-enough
or underspecification literature (Ferreira, Bailey and Ferraro 2002,
doi:10.1111/1467-8721.00158; Swets, Desmet, Clifton and Ferreira 2008, doi:10.3758/MC.36.1.201;
reviewed with mixed support by Frances 2024, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1323700), with Wonnacott
et al. (2016) supplying the child-level bridge (8- and 10-year-olds show adult good-enough
signatures, and the older children attend more to clause-marking punctuation). The rival is
therefore renamed `alt_structure_building_not_capacity` in the theory object, and a negative
posterior is now reported as consistent with capacity and experience together, which it does
not separate. Gao and Sun (2024, doi:10.1177/13670069241240936) is NOT cited for a growing
dependency-distance cost: its abstract reports the advanced group's cost rising more slowly.

**Why the line-59 capacity claim was made conditional.** The Introduction asserted that children
are disproportionately affected by dependency cost relative to adults, citing Delage and
Frauenfelder (2019), Cui et al. (2024) and Chang (2020). None compares children with adults on
a structural contrast: Delage and Frauenfelder relate spans to complex-syntax skill in 48
children aged 5 to 12 with no reading-time measure and no adult group; Cui et al. manipulated
semantic congruency in 89 Chinese children aged 9 to 12; Chang concerns executive function and
comprehension in kindergartners. Asserting the capacity conclusion one page before registering
H2 as two-sided also prejudged the adjudication. The claim is now conditional, Chang is dropped
from that sentence, and Cui et al. is cited for what it shows, reader-side modulation of online
integration. Peng et al. (2018, doi:10.1037/bul0000124) is now cited for the size of the general
relation (r = .29, and the comprehension contribution absorbed by decoding and vocabulary), and
the cross-linguistic instability of the working-memory link is recorded from Arosio et al. (2012,
doi:10.1080/15475441.2011.634691, German 7-year-olds) and Solaimani et al. (2025,
doi:10.1075/lab.24037.sol, 147 French- and German-speaking children, the correlation holding
only in French).

**Grammatical load × age band (H2).**

- Wonnacott, Joseph, Adelman and Nation (2016, doi:10.1080/17470218.2015.1011176) is the
  best-powered direct test over exactly this contrast (Year 3, 7;7–9;1, n = 42, against
  Year 6, 10;0–11;6, n = 39): a large garden-path effect with *no* age interaction (t = 0.36).
  Caveat: a temporary-ambiguity manipulation indexes reanalysis, not the sustained maintenance
  that dependency-distance measures load on, so this null does not transfer automatically.
- Joseph and Liversedge (2013, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0054141) found attenuation confined to
  late measures, at n = 14 per group. Decisive for the scale argument: expressed as a
  proportion of baseline, the complexity effect *grew* with age (roughly 15, 25 and 31 per
  cent in younger children, older children and adults). Log reading time makes coefficients
  proportional, so the experience account predicts a positive interaction here.
- Faust, Balota, Spieler and Ferraro (1999, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.777): purely
  multiplicative group slowing manufactures a spurious over-additive interaction on raw
  time. This is why the log scale is described in the protocol as load-bearing.
- The capacity account's positive evidence in this band is correlational and largely auditory
  (Delage and Frauenfelder 2019, doi:10.1075/lia.18013.del); Peng et al.'s meta-analysis
  (2018, doi:10.1037/bul0000124) found the working-memory relation to comprehension largely
  carried by decoding and vocabulary. The experience account (MacDonald and Christiansen
  2002, doi:10.1037/0033-295X.109.1.35; Montag and MacDonald 2015, doi:10.1037/xge0000054)
  derives the same developmental main effects without capacity.
- Booth, MacWhinney and Harasaki (2000, doi:10.1111/1467-8624.00203) report a non-monotonic
  pattern for relative clauses across 8 to 11, read from the abstract only; the full text
  was not obtained. Not cited for more than the possibility of non-monotonicity.

**Referential continuity × age band (H3).**

- Cain and Nash (2011, doi:10.1037/a0022824), Experiment 4: 8- and 10-year-olds gained the
  same self-paced speed-up from an appropriate connective; the age interaction on reading
  time is explicitly null. Their general discussion itself warns the sign may reverse for
  expository reading-to-learn material.
- Helder, Van Leijenhorst and van den Broek (2016, doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2016.02.008): online
  coherence-break detection did not differ between 8-to-9 and 10-to-11-year-olds.
- The child "reverse cohesion" demonstration (McNamara, Ozuru and Floyd 2011, no registered
  DOI; International Electronic Journal of Elementary Education, 4(1), 229–257) is a
  world-knowledge effect within one grade, on recall only, for narrative only, N = 65, and
  decoding skill moderated nothing. It licenses no age-based prediction.
- The skill side runs the other way: O'Reilly and McNamara (2007,
  doi:10.1080/01638530709336895) found skilled comprehenders benefited from *high* cohesion.
  Between 7 and 11 knowledge and skill rise together, so construction-integration predicts
  partial cancellation of unknown net sign.
- No study has manipulated whole-text coherence in this age band and measured reading rate.
  That cell of the literature is empty, which is stated in the protocol as the reason any
  determinate outcome is informative.
- Kleijn, Pander Maat and Sanders (2019, doi:10.1080/0163853X.2019.1605257; 20 texts,
  n = 794): causal and contrastive
  connectives raised comprehension, temporal did nothing, additive lowered it. This fixed
  the rule that connectives enter the component by relation type with causal and contrastive
  anchors.
- Length confound: standard high-cohesion versions run 20 to 40 per cent longer than their
  low-cohesion counterparts, so a whole-text duration would show cohesion slowing reading
  for arithmetic reasons. This fixed per-word time as the only outcome scale.
- Boundary confound (added 2026-09-05): dividing by words does not divide out sentence
  boundaries, and boundaries per word equal one over mean sentence length. Clause-final words
  attract longer looks and more regressions (Rayner, Kambe and Duffy 2000,
  doi:10.1080/713755934), and the cause is unsettled (Warren, White and Reichle 2009,
  doi:10.1016/j.cognition.2008.12.011, find reliable punctuation effects but no interaction
  with integrative complexity). Sentence length therefore has one pathway raising per-word
  time and one lowering it, which is why the confirmatory claim is made about the component
  and never about a single anchor.

## Why the two H3 accounts attach to different anchors (added 2026-09-05)

- The knowledge account concerns a bridging inference, which referential overlap and
  adjacent-sentence similarity spare; the cue-utilisation account is stated for a connective.
  Semantic and causal relatedness are dissociable for adult readers, with semantic relatedness
  weighing more for less skilled readers and causal relatedness for skilled ones (Todaro,
  Millis and Dandotkar 2010, doi:10.1080/01638530903253825; Wolfe, Magliano and Larsen 2005,
  doi:10.1080/0163853x.2005.9651678). The anchors need not therefore carry the same age
  interaction, and cancellation inside the component could produce an inside-region posterior.
  Hence the descriptive per-anchor interaction check, which gates nothing.
- The cue-utilisation rival was sourced to O'Reilly and McNamara (2007), an undergraduate
  cohesion-by-skill-by-knowledge comprehension study that says nothing about reaching or
  knowing a connective. Its source_doi in the theory object is now Cain, Patson and Andrews
  (2005, doi:10.1017/S0305000905007014), and the developmental premise is carried by that paper,
  by Volodina and Weinert (2020, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00814) and by Buchanan and Uccelli
  (2026, doi:10.1002/rrq.70096, 4,100 pupils, grades 4 to 6).
- The knowledge account is now derived rather than asserted: age differences in coherence
  inferencing between 6 and 15 survive equating knowledge availability, and accessibility
  matters more for the younger children (Barnes, Dennis and Haefele-Kalvaitis 1996,
  doi:10.1006/jecp.1996.0015); vocabulary is the key predictor between 6 and 10 (Currie and
  Cain 2015, doi:10.1016/j.jecp.2015.03.005); higher knowledge goes with faster reading of
  inference-demanding sentences at 11 to 12 (Cain et al. 2024, doi:10.1111/1467-9817.12474).
- A second route to a negative interaction is now registered in prose but not as a seventh
  rival: on the Landscape model a reader who brings a lower standard of coherence attempts no
  bridging inference and pays nothing for the gap (van den Broek and Helder 2017,
  doi:10.1080/0163853X.2017.1306677; van den Broek, Rapp and Kendeou 2005,
  doi:10.1080/0163853X.2005.9651685). The online evidence bounds it: children of 8 to 13 make
  the local inferences coherence requires (Joseph, Wonnacott and Nation 2021,
  doi:10.1177/1747021821999007) and both age groups slow at coherence breaks (Helder et al. 2016).
  A negative verdict is reported as consistent with cue utilisation and with a lower standard,
  which this design does not separate.
- Van den Bosch, Segers and Verhoeven (2018, doi:10.1016/j.lindif.2017.11.007) is the nearest
  online evidence inside the younger band and runs towards the knowledge account: among 8- to
  10-year-olds the missing Dutch causal connective lengthened processing only for
  second-language readers with weaker syntactic knowledge.
- "Reverse cohesion effect" is no longer used as the label for a positive continuity
  coefficient on time. The effect is a learning outcome measured on recall, questions and
  sorting (McNamara and Kintsch 1996, doi:10.1080/01638539609544975; McNamara et al. 1996;
  O'Reilly and McNamara 2007), and its construction-integration mechanism predicts *slower*
  reading of the less cohesive text, so a faster-reading result is not the same phenomenon.
- Cohesion is distinguished from coherence in the passage-level paragraph, with the
  argument-overlap ancestry of referential overlap (Kintsch and van Dijk 1978,
  doi:10.1037/0033-295X.85.5.363), the vector-space approximation to it (Foltz, Kintsch and
  Landauer 1998, doi:10.1080/01638539809545029) and the marker-speeds-processing result
  (Sanders and Noordman 2000, doi:10.1207/S15326950dp2901_3).
- Discourse-family tie-break: referential and deep cohesion are separable dimensions (Graesser,
  McNamara and Kulikowich 2011, doi:10.3102/0013189X11413260) and deep cohesion carried the
  unique variance in the closest child sample (Pickren et al. 2022), so where the family retains
  more than one component the one loading highest on the causal and contrastive connectives is
  the confirmatory one.

## Why Hypothesis 4 carries a compression diagnostic and a non-binding-audio subset

- The selective-decoding account has the mechanism evidence: a partial read-aloud
  accommodation helped poor decoders while a pacing-only control did nothing (Giusto and
  Ehri 2019, doi:10.1177/0022219418789377; full text checked 2026-09-04: 82 third-graders,
  28 poor decoders assigned 9/10/9 to read-aloud, pacing-only and standard conditions;
  poor decoders scored 36.3 vs 28.2 vs 28.2 on the Gates-MacGinitie, Bonferroni RA > P = S,
  d = 1.53 read-aloud vs standard and -0.03 pacing vs standard; average decoders gained
  nothing, d = -0.02, and pacing alone d = -0.39); the benefit concentrates in poor decoding with
  intact language (Keelor et al. 2023, doi:10.1007/s11881-023-00281-9); the time saved falls
  on first encounters with orthographically novel words and is gone by the second
  (Valentini et al. 2024, doi:10.1002/rrq.522).
- The pacing account has the outcome evidence: the reading-while-listening benefit is
  confined to externally paced comparisons (Clinton-Lisell 2023, no registered DOI, OSF
  10.31219/osf.io/ku6tv; Clinton-Lisell 2022, doi:10.3102/00346543211060871); a preregistered
  mechanism test found comprehension worse under narration, attributed to the forced pace
  blocking rereading (Hui and Godfroid 2026, doi:10.1111/lang.12721); and measured
  incidentally, narration collapsed the standard deviation of exposure time from 11.56 s to
  0.62 s (Valentini et al. 2024).
- Nobody has regressed reading time on decoding-level and integration-level predictors
  separately under both narration states. H4 is that decomposition; the residual-variance
  ratio and the faster-than-audio subset are the two controls that keep the pacing account
  honest.
- **The pacing rival was derived wrongly (corrected 2026-09-05).** Equal proportional
  attenuation follows only if the recording's duration per word is constant across texts, and
  it is not: a spoken rendition runs longer per word where words carry more syllables and where
  clause and sentence boundaries add pauses. Mean orthographic length anchors LexicalDifficulty
  and mean sentence length anchors GrammaticalLoad, so under pure pacing the narrated lexical
  slope stays positive through the audio itself while the narrated continuity slope goes to
  zero, which pushes the registered difference at or below zero rather than to it. Reading
  aloud runs at about 183 wpm against 238 to 260 silent, with the gap between text types
  predicted by word length (Brysbaert 2019, doi:10.1016/j.jml.2019.104047), and audio support
  lengthened reading time without changing comprehension in secondary students with and without
  dyslexia (Knoop-van Campen et al. 2021, doi:10.1007/s11881-021-00246-w). Hence the predicted
  spoken duration per word, computed from the held texts before any outcome is joined, and the
  registered duration-adjusted sensitivity contrast. The below-minus-0.05 cell no longer reads
  as reverse selectivity alone.
- **A third account is now stated: the child listens and does not read.** Preschoolers' fixations
  during shared reading fall overwhelmingly on the illustrations (Evans and Saint-Aubin 2005,
  doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2005.01636.x), print attention rises across the primary grades with
  skill (Roy-Charland, Saint-Aubin and Evans 2007, doi:10.1007/s11145-007-9059-9), and in
  electronic storybooks 4- to 5-year-olds spent 19 per cent of their time on print, rising to
  38 per cent with synchronised highlighting (Liao et al. 2020,
  doi:10.1016/j.compedu.2019.103703). In older children reading in a second language, audio
  moved the eyes to the pictures without changing comprehension (Pellicer-Sánchez et al. 2020,
  doi:10.1017/S0272263120000091; Serrano and Pellicer-Sánchez 2022), whereas 8- to 9-year-olds
  did fixate the words (Valentini et al. 2024). The logs cannot separate a paced reading from a
  listening, so an inside-region verdict is reported as time set by the recording and never as
  evidence that narrated reading was reading. Question (a) to the data controller now also asks
  about synchronised highlighting and automatic page advance, and we will observe both in the
  app ourselves.
- **The sparing is conditional on relative rate.** Adults' eyes run ahead of the audio in
  reading while listening (Conklin et al. 2020, doi:10.1177/0267658320921496), as the eye leads
  the voice in reading aloud (Laubrock and Kliegl 2015, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01432), and
  adult learners read slightly more slowly with audio (Malone et al. 2025,
  doi:10.1111/lang.70007), whereas 8- to 9-year-olds pattern the other way (Valentini et al.
  2024). So the decoding account describes readers slower than the recording and the pacing
  account readers faster than it, and a determinate verdict reports which regime dominates.
  p8's mechanism in the theory object now says so, and the within-band contrast is exploratory
  with no decision rule.

## Why the plausibility bounds are 10 and 400 words per minute

- The only published precedent stating such a rule with its yield: Ripoll Salceda, Tapia
  Montesinos and Aguado Alonso (2020, doi:10.1016/j.psicod.2020.01.002; English edition
  doi:10.1016/j.psicoe.2020.01.001; full text checked 2026-09-04) deleted silent rates above
  400 wpm as exceeding adult capability and indicating skipped text, citing Rayner, Schotter,
  Masson, Potter and Treiman (2016, doi:10.1177/1529100615623267) for the ceiling, and
  removed 313 cases from tests assessing 19,237 participants (1.6 per cent); the rule bit on
  silent measures only. Their pooled child norms also anchor the lower bound, with a caveat
  the manuscript now states: the slowest pooled mean is 30.48 wpm (grade 1, silent, K = 2,
  CI 9.56 to 51.4), but the normal range they propose for that grade (mean +/- median SD,
  their Table 5) is 8 to 53 wpm, and 41 to 117 for grade 2, so a floor of 10 wpm can touch
  the slowest first-grade silent readers and is a pure "session left open" criterion only
  from second grade.
- Context norms: Hasbrouck and Tindal's compiled oral-fluency norms (2017, ERIC ED594994);
  silent comprehension-based rates rising from about 115 wpm in grade 2 (Hiebert, Samuels
  and Rasinski 2012, doi:10.1080/19388071.2010.531887), with the 2011 replication finding
  substantially lower rates than 1960 (Spichtig et al. 2016, doi:10.1002/rrq.137). Adult
  silent rates across languages fall roughly in 181 to 285 wpm, which motivated 160 wpm as a
  conservative ceiling on any narration rate in H4's non-binding-audio subset.

## What the 2026-09-05 theory pass added, and why (lexical, developmental, metacognitive, naturalistic, cross-linguistic)

**Word level.** Surprisal is one of the three lexical anchors but was never motivated in the prose.
The paragraph now states surprisal theory (Hale 2001, doi:10.3115/1073336.1073357; Levy 2008), the
linear linking function (Smith and Levy 2013; Shain et al. 2024, doi:10.1073/pnas.2307876121) and its
cross-linguistic test in eleven languages (Wilcox et al. 2023, doi:10.1162/tacl_a_00612). Linearity is
what licenses a whole-text mean of per-token surprisal as the text-level summary, which is the quantity
this design uses. Frequency and predictability dissociate and are additive (Shain 2024,
doi:10.1162/opmi_a_00119), so the pooled component indexes both. Surprisal is placed in the lexical
family because its purchase on reading times is carried by rare and unexpected words (Oh, Yue and
Schuler 2024, doi:10.18653/v1/2024.eacl-long.162) and because a large benchmark found no evidence that
it explains syntactic disambiguation difficulty (Huang et al. 2024, doi:10.1016/j.jml.2024.104510).
The 1 GB Goldfish tier is now also defended psychometrically (de Varda and Marelli 2023; Boeve and
Bogaerts 2025, doi:10.3758/s13428-025-02774-4). The Lexical Quality Hypothesis (Perfetti 2007;
Perfetti and Stafura 2014, doi:10.1080/10888438.2013.827687) is named as the account that generates
p1, p4 and p5 at once, which is what makes H1 and H6 tests of one account at two grains. The theory
object's pred_h1_lexical evidence row 10.1002/rrq.498 was WRONG: it resolves to Mezière et al. (2023),
an adult eye-tracking study, not to Ludewig et al.; it is replaced by Kuperman et al. 2024
(10.1016/j.jml.2023.104497) and Wilcox et al. 2023, both reading-time evidence for a reading-time
prediction. Concreteness lost the irrelevant memory clause and gained a second reading-time source
(Yao et al. 2024, doi:10.1080/02699931.2024.2367062). Lexical diversity gained its mechanism
(repetition lowers reading time: Joseph and Nation 2018, doi:10.1016/j.jecp.2017.08.010; Joseph et al.
2014) and the caveat that passage-level type-token ratios were among the measures Kuperman et al.
(2025) found negligible.

**Developmental framework.** The Introduction asserted that the age band stands for automaticity
without naming a theory. It now states the simple view (Gough and Tunmer 1986; Hoover and Gough 1990;
Lonigan et al. 2018), automaticity (LaBerge and Samuels 1974, doi:10.1016/0010-0285(74)90015-2),
lexical quality, the reading systems framework and self-teaching, with the eye-movement record
(Tiffin-Richards and Schroeder 2015; Huestegge et al. 2009, doi:10.1016/j.visres.2009.09.012) and the
open question whether the change is reading level or oculomotor maturation (Justino and Kolinsky 2023,
doi:10.1016/j.actpsy.2023.103927). The bottleneck-release argument matters most: on a log outcome, a
grammatical increment divided by a shrinking decoding base gives a LARGER proportional slope in the
older band, so a positive H2 interaction is the automaticity account's signature on this scale and not
an artefact of the transformation. The same dilution predicts a negative H3 interaction and a
negative-or-null lexical interaction, which is now reported as a joint pattern.

**Outcome construct.** The inference from whole-story elapsed time to processing demand was never
defended. It now cites the eye-mind assumption (Just and Carpenter 1980), passage-level evidence
(Nahatame and Yamaguchi 2025, doi:10.1111/lang.70011; Amendum et al. 2018) and names the registered
engagement rival with a source (Welland and Burns 2025, doi:10.1177/08295735251342678: in 122
sixth-graders, interest accounted for more variance in time on task than passage difficulty), whose
source_doi was previously null in the theory object. A second dilution is now stated: part of any
whole-story interval is illustrations and page turns, and print attention rises across the primary
grades (Roy-Charland et al. 2007), so a smaller base in the older band could be less time away from
the text rather than faster decoding, and these data cannot separate the two.

**Selection.** The boundary drawn on Meng's objection was stated loosely. It now uses the structural
criterion (Hernan et al. 2004, doi:10.1097/01.ede.0000135174.63482.43; Elwert and Winship 2014; Lamp
and MacKinnon 2025), distinguishes reader-only gates, which change whom a slope describes, from
collider gates, which distort it, and names completion as the only gate meeting the narrower
condition. The completion collider is no longer treated as neutral between H2 and H3: differential
attrition by band pushes the grammatical interaction positive and the continuity interaction negative,
so a verdict of either of those signs is reported as consistent also with attrition, and the
completion rate by age band is reported beside it. Cao et al. (2026, doi:10.1287/msom.2024.1527)
supplies field evidence that completion in a children's reading app varies with resources and demand.
Blind analysis is named as the methodology the sequestration implements (MacCoun and Perlmutter 2015;
Dutilh et al. 2021).

**Generated text.** The importance of the study rested on an empty Scopus cell, which reads as a
priority claim in numerical dress. The count is now support for a stated claim: a generator need not
distribute text properties as human authorship does (Reinhart et al. 2025,
doi:10.1073/pnas.2422455122; Munoz-Ortiz et al. 2024; Doshi and Hauser 2024,
doi:10.1126/sciadv.adn5290), so whether the laboratory account describes reading over such a
population is a question about its scope (Haeffel and Cobb 2022). Direct comparisons of generated and
human-written text are now cited (Appel et al. 2025; Ripoll y Schmitz and Sonnleitner 2025; Valentini
et al. 2023, whose finding that models could not hold vocabulary to a younger level bears on the
lexical component).

**Cross-linguistic.** The theory now says what should and should not vary: direction in every language
(Frost 2012; Verhoeven and Perfetti 2022), magnitude expected to vary for the lexical component with
the script and for the grammatical component with word order and statistics, and no derived reason for
referential continuity. The simplification order under Decision rules is justified by that ranking.
Korean's per-word quantities are eojeol quantities throughout (Baek, Choi and Gordon 2023,
doi:10.1177/17470218221104736), which affects the outcome's denominator, the plausibility bounds, mean
sentence length and mean dependency distance, not only the norm coverage; the syllable block is the
unit Korean children acquire first (Cho 2009), and length may not carry the lexical component in
Hangul at all (Pae, Bae and Yi 2020). The Greek CHILDES claim was WRONG and is deleted; Greek is
instead characterised by measured transparency (Protopapas and Vlahou 2009) and by fluency predicting
accuracy by grade 2 (Georgiou et al. 2025). The reading language need not be the child's first
language (Whitford and Titone 2012; Kuperman et al. 2023, MECO L2), which the country-by-language
sensitivity analysis is the only purchase on.

**Metacognition.** The bibliography held no metacognition literature at all. H5's link between reading
time and the rating now rests on the effort heuristic of the cue-utilisation account (Koriat 1997;
Undorf and Erdfelder 2011) with meta-analytic sizes (Baars et al. 2020, r = -.36; David et al. 2024,
beta = -.19) and the qualification that decides its application here: effort the text imposes is
attributed to the text, effort the reader chooses is not (Koriat et al. 2006, 2014), and this app is
voluntary and self-paced. The ordering clause is no longer presented as near-automatic: accessibility
of cues differs across the three demands, and a cohesion manipulation moved paragraph-level but not
whole-text judgements unless the text remained available (Lefevre and Lories 2004). "No published
study has" was replaced by a statement of what the searches located. The age band now bears on H5
explicitly, because judging by effort is itself acquired over exactly these bands (Koriat et al. 2009).
Scale-use biases in children are recorded (Mellor and Moore 2014; Borgers et al. 2004; Zaman et al.
2013; Read and Horton 2025) with the direction registered as unknown, and the category marginals are
reported before fitting, which adds a sixth registered design-analysis adjustment. The third
d-separation implication is flagged in advance as the one the literature most expects to fail
(Efklides 1999, 2006; Torres and Toplak 2026; Thiede et al. 2010).

**Practice.** The H6 unit was ambiguous across three scales (per doubling, per SD, ROPE per SD); it is
now log(1 + words), standardised, with the doubling reported as the interpretable contrast. The
account is renamed orthographic learning and sourced. Item-based transfer bounds how far it can reach
(Tucker and Deacon 2025; Li and Wang 2023, doi:10.1080/00461520.2022.2137673), and Share's
combinatorial model (2025, doi:10.1002/rrq.603) predicts a shallower speed-up where decoding
automatised earlier. The account-mean coefficient is now stated to be non-diagnostic, since both
accounts predict it negative, with the twin evidence for the reverse path (van Bergen et al. 2018,
2023; Erbeli et al. 2020). The within-window acyclicity assumption is argued rather than asserted, with
two named routes (attrition within a child's series; drift in material chosen) and two registered
non-gating refits. Each covariate is now tied to a named alternative, and maturation is sized
(Christ et al. 2010; Khanolainen et al. 2024).

## The prior published analysis of Let's Story, and why it leaves the hypotheses untested

Ratner and Murphy (2026, doi:10.3389/fdpys.2026.1816559) analysed this platform. The author
confirmed the identification on 2026-09-05; the abstract does not name it. Full text checked the
same day. What it did:

- 419,700 story sessions, 18 languages, 1 to 30 November 2024, the first thirty days after launch.
- Children aged 4 to 9, in bands 4-5, 5-7 and 7-9.
- AGGREGATED DASHBOARD counts, not session-level records. Its authors state this "precludes
  inference about individual learners' experiences, intentions, or learning outcomes" and prevents
  tracking a learner across sessions.
- Variables: completion, the point in a story at which a session was abandoned, dictionary and
  discussion scaffold use, language, and the post-reading difficulty rating.
- Rating present in 155,700 sessions: just right 46.91 per cent (73,045), too easy 37.42 per cent
  (58,271), too difficult 15.67 per cent (24,399). Availability about 37 per cent.
- Descriptive statistics only. No inferential statistics, no modelling, no hypothesis testing.
- NO text property, readability measure or linguistic feature analysed. NO reading duration,
  session length or reading speed analysed.
- Limitations its authors name: aggregation, no record of adult mediation, ratings "indicative
  rather than representative", and novelty effects in the first month after launch.

Why it does not overlap this protocol: every registered association here relates a measured text
property to a behavioural outcome, and it relates none; the outcome here is reading duration per
word, which it does not analyse; and tier 2 needs session-level records tied to an account, which it
did not hold. Its window (Nov 2024) does not overlap the one here (opening 1 January 2026), so its
novelty caveat applies to it and much less to this study.

What it does supply, and how it is used: the rating's availability and category proportions are
marginals of one column, carrying no predictor-outcome association. They are declared in the
Methods blindness convention and used as registered inputs to the third and fourth design-analysis
adjustments, which can now be run before submission on published values and re-run on the extract's
own figures. The Scopus count did not return this paper although it was published two months before
the search, so the count is now reported as a sizing of one database and not as a claim about what
exists.

## Computational-linguistics corrections, 2026-09-05 (surprisal)

Checked directly against the resources' own pages during the pass, not taken on trust.

- **The feature was defined on the wrong denominator.** The protocol registered "mean per-token
  surprisal". Surprisal theory's linearity, and every result licensing a text-level mean, is stated
  for words. Mean per-token surprisal is (total surprisal / words) / tokens-per-word, and
  tokens-per-word rises with rare, long and morphologically complex words, which is exactly the
  material LexicalDifficulty indexes. Dividing by tokens therefore deflates the feature in the texts
  where it should be highest, unevenly within a language and worse across the ten, whose token
  premiums differ intrinsically. The feature is now mean per-WORD surprisal, a word's surprisal being
  the sum of its subword tokens' surprisals, over the same whitespace-delimited words that form the
  outcome's denominator (eojeol in Korean, as everywhere in this protocol). Changed in Materials, in
  the Introduction and in c_lexical_accessibility's measurement list.
- **mGPT cannot serve as the surprisal sensitivity model, because it does not cover Dutch.** Verified
  twice on 2026-09-05, from the Hugging Face card's language metadata and the project README: 61
  languages, 25 families, including Afrikaans, Danish and Swedish but NOT Dutch. Dutch is one of the
  ten and is the language with the published psychometric evaluation of surprisal against reading
  times (Boeve and Bogaerts 2025), so the registered sensitivity analysis could not have run where it
  would carry most information. Replaced by two models, each holding one thing constant:
  EuroLLM-1.7B (`utter-project/EuroLLM-1.7B`, 1.657 B parameters, Apache-2.0, 4,096-token context;
  all ten languages confirmed from its card on 2026-09-05) for the single-multilingual-model
  contrast, and `goldfish-models/<lang>_100mb` for the data-quantity contrast.
- **Goldfish identifiers now recorded exactly**, as the protocol's fourth rule promises: 124,770,816
  parameters, GPT-2 configuration, 50,000-token SentencePiece vocabulary, 512-token window, ten
  epochs, OSCAR 21.09, Apache-2.0. The "identical 125-million-parameter architecture" claim holds at
  the 100 MB and 1 GB tiers only; the 5 MB and 10 MB tiers are 4-layer models of about 36 to 39
  million parameters. The byte-premium scaling equalises bytes, not tokens: premiums run 1.000
  (English) to 1.293 (Korean) and 1.967 (Greek), and training tokens 210.8 to 251.4 million.
- **The tier is now defended positively.** Inverse scaling alone favours the smallest model, so it
  does not select 1 GB over 100 MB. The added argument is the ~2-billion-training-token optimum,
  which the 1 GB models meet at 2.11 to 2.51 billion over ten epochs while the smaller tiers fall
  below it. The de Varda and Marelli range (564 M to 4.5 B) does not reach 125 M, and their
  late-measure result runs the other way, which the cell now states.
- **A 512-token scoring rule is registered**, since Goldfish's window is 512 tokens and the texts run
  to 400 words with high subword fertility in Greek and Korean: 512-token windows at a stride of 256,
  retaining a token only where it has 256 tokens of preceding context.
- **The frozen-snapshot criterion is now applied consistently.** The supplement rejected `wordfreq`
  partly for being frozen to about 2021, then adopted Goldfish, trained wholly on OSCAR 21.09 from
  the February 2021 Common Crawl. The asymmetry is defensible and favourable, and is now argued: a
  2021 snapshot is a defect in a frequency norm meant to index current usage and a safeguard in a
  predictability model applied to machine-generated stimuli, because it predates the generator.
- **Proper names were an unmeasured, reader-selected ingredient of every generated text.** The family
  chooses a hero, a companion and a setting, and those are named in the story, so proper nouns vary
  systematically with a choice the reader made. No subtitle frequency list, age-of-acquisition norm or
  concreteness norm contains an invented name, so leaving proper nouns in the coverage denominator
  would fail a language for a reason unrelated to its norms, and leaving them in a norm-based mean
  would bias LexicalDifficulty, the component H1 rests on. Proper nouns are now excluded from the
  denominator and from every norm-based mean, each mean is reported with and without the exclusion,
  and three length-normalised name quantities are reported. Surprisal keeps the names in context and
  excludes them only from the mean.
- **The theory object's five-sentence floor was not enforced anywhere.** c_referential_continuity
  carries "texts long enough to contain at least five sentences" as a boundary condition, but no rule
  in the sampling plan or the dimension reduction applied it. A two-sentence text yields one adjacent
  pair and a meaningless continuity score. The floor is now a per-text admission rule, and the
  distinct-text counts the precision thresholds are read against are the counts after it applies,
  which matters because a text coefficient's precision is bounded by exactly that number.
- **Narrativity entered every confirmatory model as a covariate with no definition at all.** It had no
  resource, no row in Supplementary Table 3 and nothing to freeze under rule four. Its usual source is
  scored relative to an English reference corpus and cannot be transported to ten languages. It is now
  a stipulated composite of past-tense marking, personal pronouns and direct speech, computed from the
  same parse in all ten and frozen with the inventory.
- **"Proportion of adjacent sentence pairs sharing a referring expression" implied coreference**, which
  no system delivers uniformly across these ten. It is now defined as lemma overlap in nominal
  positions, the argument-overlap rule implemented on lemmas, with pronoun-inclusive overlap as the
  sensitivity measure.
- **The similarity encoders were the wrong way round.** BGE-M3 is presented by its authors for
  retrieval, and the feature is sentence-to-sentence similarity, on which multilingual-E5-large-instruct
  is the stronger. The two are swapped and the per-language correlation is reported.
- **Affective coverage was overstated as existence.** Native valence and arousal sets are about a
  thousand items in six of the ten, which cannot match 80 per cent of a story's tokens, so the native
  sensitivity analysis is expected to run in three or four languages only. The induced resource's own
  gold evaluation covers eight of these ten (not French, not Korean), with arousal correlating as low
  as about .50 in Greek, part of which is criterion noise since independent native sets agree less on
  arousal than on valence. Every affective value here descends from adult ratings, which is now stated.
- **MTLD is least stable at exactly these text lengths.** The moving-average type-token ratio over a
  50-token window is now the primary diversity anchor, with MTLD reported beside it.
- **Two different 80 per cent rules were both called "the 80 per cent coverage rule".** One counts
  matched tokens within a language, the other counts texts receiving a valid match, and they can give
  opposite verdicts on the same feature. They are now named separately and their scopes stated.
- **Stanza's published accuracy table lags its software.** The performance page still reports scores
  "as of version 1.5.1 on UD 2.12" (every LAS figure in Supplementary Table 3 was re-checked against
  it on 2026-09-05 and matches), while the current release is 1.14.0 (July 2026), for which no
  per-treebank table is published. The supplement now says which version will be run and what will be
  recorded.

## Why Hypothesis 5 treats the rating as a measure under test

- No peer-reviewed study was found in which children aged 6 to 12 rated the difficulty of a
  text and the rating was validated against the text's properties; the child metacognition
  literature rates confidence in answers, judgements of learning, or enjoyment.
- The three-point easy / just-right / difficult format is a classroom heuristic with no
  published psychometrics.
- An evidence sweep reported an ordinal alpha of 0.23 for adolescents rating sentence
  complexity, attributed to Thome et al. (2025); the primary record could not be located in
  Crossref, so that figure was excluded from the manuscript and the argument rests on the
  absence of validation evidence alone. Hence the text-level aggregation, the five-rater
  floor, and the pre-registered three-way interpretation grid in which Hypothesis 1
  disambiguates a failure.
- Superseded 2026-09-05. The first two bullets stated the absence as a priority claim ("no
  published study has"), which the journal's Stage 1 rules exclude. The adjacent literature is
  larger than they allowed: children of 8 to 10 rate mental effort in a way that tracks task
  complexity (Baars et al. 2018), children in both bands judge their own comprehension of texts
  with calibration improving between grades 2 and 4 (Steiner et al. 2020; Mirandola et al. 2018;
  Markovich et al. 2026), adults' judgements about a text track manipulated coherence (Rawson and
  Dunlosky 2002), comprehensibility ratings of narrative text are predictable from computed
  features (Tilmatine et al. 2024), and self-reported difficulty has been recovered from tutoring
  logs (Janning et al. 2016). The claim now made is about what the searches located, and the
  psychometric gap for the three-point format is stated as a gap in what was found.

## Why the multilingual resource plan changed

- wordfreq is frozen at roughly 2021 and was discontinued in September 2024, its maintainer
  citing generated-text contamination of post-2022 web corpora; its Korean and Greek lists
  are thinner than the rest. Replaced as primary by subs2vec (van Paridon and Thompson 2021,
  doi:10.3758/s13428-020-01406-3), one pipeline over 55 languages.
- The non-English NRC VAD lexicons are 2022 machine translations of the English norms under
  a non-commercial licence; replaced by MEmoLon (Buechel et al. 2020,
  doi:10.18653/v1/2020.acl-main.112), induced uniformly for 91 languages.
- LaBSE is optimised for translation-pair retrieval, not within-language similarity;
  replaced by BGE-M3 (Chen et al. 2024, doi:10.18653/v1/2024.findings-acl.137).
- Goldfish (doi:10.63317/5ceec3hhv4d5) trains an identical architecture on byte-premium-scaled
  data per language, which is what makes surprisal comparable across languages by
  construction; mGPT is the single-model sensitivity.
- Korean lacks a subtitle frequency norm and any coverage in the Łuniewska cross-linguistic
  age-of-acquisition set. Its indexed rated norms are small: 450 nouns rated for concreteness
  (Hong et al. 2016), four valence and arousal sets of 252 to 450 words (Lee et al. 2022,
  CogSci), and 665 nouns rated for imageability (Park 2004). (Corrected 25 September 2026: this
  entry had said Korean had no indexed concreteness or native affect norms.) Crossref
  indexes Korean-language journals poorly, so this is recorded as a search limitation and a
  KCI/RISS search by a Korean-reading collaborator is required before the feature inventory
  is frozen.
