Supplementary material: Text properties and children’s reading pace in a multilingual storytelling app

S1. The theory object

The constructs, causal claims, auxiliary assumptions, rival accounts and predictions behind this protocol are held in a single machine-readable file, theory/intract.theory.yaml, which the theoryforge package reads and checks (Bernabeu, 2026b). Figure 2 of the main text is emitted from that file, so the diagram cannot disagree with the hypotheses. Running theory/build_theory.R reproduces everything in this section.

The object registers twelve constructs, each with a definition, its intended measurement and its boundary conditions. Eighteen propositions link them, each carrying the mechanism it claims and not only a direction. Fifteen of the eighteen are directed edges of the causal graph, and the other three are moderation claims. Six auxiliary assumptions are recorded as entries in their own right, five of them corresponding to questions put to the data controller and listed in Table 2 of the main text. Six rival accounts are registered, and seven of the ten predictions are marked diagnostic against one of them.

The rigour checklist scores the object at 90.9 out of 100 with no blocking failure, and every item passes. On falsifiability and precision, at least one prediction forbids an observation, all ten predictions are interval predictions, and their mean severity clears the threshold. On construct quality, every construct carries a definition, a measurement and boundary conditions, no pair exceeds the lexical redundancy screen (largest observed similarity 0.14), and the theory’s scope is stated. On structure, every proposition states a mechanism, at least one relation is causal, auxiliary assumptions are fewer than propositions with none added defensively in response to a prediction, and every prediction is reachable from a proposition in the graph. Finally, at least one prediction discriminates between registered alternatives, and a formal-model reference points at the design specification used for the precision analysis.

Because the causal graph is acyclic it yields testable implications, thirty of them, of which three are tested in the fourth outcome-neutral quality check of the main text. The most consequential is that the three text properties should be mutually independent once the reading level and the generation route are conditioned on. A first version of the object, lacking the text-provenance construct, implied that they were independent unconditionally, which is false of any real corpus: a text’s target reading level shifts all three property families at once. Adding text provenance as a common cause of all three, and giving it an incoming path from the reader’s own decoding automaticity, both repaired that implication and derived the adjustment set the analysis uses.

S3. Language resources for the text features

A multilingual design faces a measurement problem that a monolingual one does not, and it is not solved anywhere in the literature. Concreteness taken from a 40,000-item English rating study, a 350,000-lemma German regression, a 1,400-item Spanish rating study and a 77-language embedding prediction are four different measurements wearing one label. If a resource is chosen language by language on the grounds of local quality, then the method of measurement is perfectly confounded with the language, and standardising within language removes the difference in location and scale but not the difference in construct. There are two clean responses: use one uniformly produced resource everywhere, accepting lower absolute quality for higher comparability, or use native norms and treat every cross-language comparison as descriptive. This protocol takes the first, because language enters the confirmatory models as a grouping factor with varying slopes, and those slopes have to mean the same thing in every language. The native-norm sensitivity analysis and the per-language correlation between the two measures are specified in the main text. That correlation is the quantity that tells a reader how much the choice mattered.

Two consequences of that rule revise what an earlier version of this protocol assumed. The wordfreq package is not used as the primary frequency source: its data are a frozen snapshot of usage to about 2021, its maintainer discontinued it in September 2024 to avoid ingesting post-2022 web text contaminated by generated language, and its wordlists for Korean and Greek are built from fewer sources than the rest. The non-English editions of the NRC Valence, Arousal and Dominance lexicon are not used at all: they are machine translations of the English norms made in 2022, not ratings collected in the target languages, and their licence is non-commercial.

Table 3 gives the primary resource for each feature family, the sensitivity resource, and the coverage across the ten languages the structural sample showed. Where the export turns out to carry more languages, the same rules are applied to them and the table is extended to cover them.

Korean is the weak point, and we record it here in advance. Of the seven norm-based feature families, Korean lacks a subtitle frequency norm, any coverage in the cross-linguistic age-of-acquisition set, any indexed concreteness or imageability norms, and any native valence and arousal norms. What it does have is a uniform frequency estimate, a Goldfish model, both parsers, induced affect and a lexical decision megastudy of 30,930 words. Korean therefore contributes to the lexical and grammatical components and to the affective predictors through the uniform resources, and contributes to no analysis that requires a native norm. A search of Crossref is a poor instrument for Korean-language psycholinguistics, so before the features are frozen a Korean-reading collaborator will search the Korea Citation Index and RISS directly, and any norm set found there will be added to the sensitivity analysis with the addition recorded as a deviation from this protocol. Greek is the next weakest: it has no native concreteness set for Standard Modern Greek, no CHILDES collection, and an affective lexicon of a different character from the rated norms available elsewhere.

Every tool is recorded by name, version and, where the tool is a model, by the exact checkpoint. Several of the tools named here have no registered DOI, which is normal for research software distributed through model repositories. Those are identified by checkpoint instead, and the identifiers are deposited with the feature-extraction code.

S5. The design analysis in full

The precision analysis simulated the planned crossed structure from the portable design specifications deposited in design_analysis/, and design_analysis/run_design_analysis.R reproduces it (Bernabeu, 2026a). Full results for every grid point, including the Monte Carlo standard error and Wilson bounds on each decision probability and the counts of converged, singular and warning fits, are in design_analysis/precision_sweep.csv. Figure 2 shows the narration and within-child curves that the main text summarises.

The ordinal outcome behind Hypothesis 5 is handled by a separate script, design_analysis/run_ordinal_analysis.R, because a cumulative logit cannot be fitted by the routine the timing models use and treating a three-category rating as continuous would answer a different question. The script keeps the same design specification and the same seed discipline, generates the rating from it, retains each reading with the 30 per cent availability the structural sample showed, applies the protocol’s five-rater floor inside every replicate, and fits the model the protocol specifies with ordinal::clmm.

The result is the most demanding requirement in the design. At 200, 400, 800 and 1,600 generated texts, with twelve readings each, the 95 per cent interval on a coefficient whose true value is 0.10 log odds has a mean width of 0.44, 0.30, 0.21 and 0.15, and the probability of a determinate verdict never rises above 0.29. The target of 0.90 is therefore not reached within the range simulated, and no crossing is quoted from it. What the grid does establish is the scaling: the fitted exponents of interval width on the number of texts are between -0.51 and -0.53 against a theoretical -0.5, and on that law the requirement projects to roughly 8,000 to 8,700 generated texts, which is about 2,300 to 2,500 texts carrying at least five ratings. Set against the 738 texts the same coefficient needs on reading time, the rating demands an order of magnitude more, which is what a three-category response observed for 30 per cent of readings costs. Both quantities depend on the rating’s availability across the full window and on how heavily texts are reused, which the provider-computed counts will supply, and the simulation is re-run at the observed values before any outcome model is fitted. design_analysis/summarise_ordinal.R pools the cached grid points and reproduces every number in this paragraph.

S6. Deriving the priors from the systematic review

Priors for the confirmatory main effects come from the systematic review accompanying this project, through the pipeline whose freeze-and-run discipline is stated under Priors in the main text. This section records what the main text does not: the mapping from the review’s extracted effects to this protocol’s coefficients, the conversion order, and the pooling and de-duplication rules. The mapping is one-to-many and is fixed as follows. LexicalDifficulty pools the review’s word-frequency, lexical-coverage, other-lexical and lexical-diversity effects on reading, sign-harmonised so that frequency and coverage are flipped to a difficulty direction while age of acquisition, word length and surprisal keep their sign. GrammaticalLoad pools syntactic-complexity and sentence-length effects. ReferentialContinuity pools coherence effects, with discourse-structure rows informing the secondary narrativity predictor instead. Valence and arousal pool the text-emotionality rows separately where the extraction distinguishes them.

Estimates are put on a common scale in a fixed order of preference. A standardised beta or a correlation goes through Fisher’s z. A d or g converts to r using the effective sample size, and an odds ratio converts to d and then to r. Where no effect size is reported but a test statistic is, r is recovered from t and its degrees of freedom and signed by the reported direction. Unstandardised coefficients without convertible scale information are not pooled and are listed in an exclusion appendix, as are rows whose direction the extraction could not resolve. Pooling is a random-effects meta-analysis within each combination of coefficient and outcome class, with one estimate per paper per relation, so that labelled subgroups of a single paper are never treated as independent. Where a meta-analysis in the corpus subsumes three or more of the corpus’s own primary studies, the pooled row is preferred and the overlapping primaries dropped; otherwise the primaries are used and the pooled row dropped, and the alternative is reported as a sensitivity analysis.

The prior is then Student’s \(t(3, \hat\mu, \sigma)\) with \(\hat\mu\) the pooled estimate on the model’s scale and \(\sigma = 2\sqrt{\mathrm{SE}(\hat\mu)^2 + \hat\tau^2}\), floored at 0.10. The heavy tails let the data override a misplaced centre. The complete prior table, the forest plots, the conversion arithmetic and the list of excluded rows will accompany the Stage 2 report, and the pipeline’s output is timestamped and deposited before the extract is transferred.

  3. It will be transferred only on written confirmation of in-principle acceptance.
  4. Material shared with the team to date: a 35-row sample and the data dictionary, shared on 27 July 2026 to confirm the structure of the table; the story texts; and the counts and file record described above.

Owner: [name, role]
Deputy: [name, role]
Signed: [name, role, date]

Two points arise from clause 4. The 35-row sample carries real reading times, and the main text discloses it directly. The story texts are named here because the editorial office confirmed in advance that stimulus materials may be received before submission, for the reasons given under Data provenance in the main text.
